## Finding your active deals

When you log in, your dashboard shows all projects currently in the Pitching, DD, Closing, and Completed stages. Projects needing your attention — ones with new activity, pending votes, or unread notes — are highlighted.

Click any project to open it.

## The project view

A project in AngelHub has several tabs. Here is what each one contains:

**Summary**
The entrepreneur's application — their answers to the application form, company overview, funding ask, and any attached documents. This is the starting point. Read it in full before looking at anything else.

**Screening**
The Assessor's notes from the screening process — what they found, what questions they had, and their overall assessment of pitch readiness. Useful context before a pitch.

**Coverage map**
An 11-topic grid showing DD progress. Green topics have been addressed, amber are partially covered, red have not yet been investigated. The coverage map gives you a quick read on where the DD is and where the gaps are.

**Work packages**
The detailed DD findings across the 8 work packages. Each work package covers a specific dimension of the business — team, market, product, financials, legal, and so on. Read the work packages that are most relevant to your own expertise or your investment decision.

**Notes**
Your private notes on this deal. Visible only to you (and, on Scale plan, notes you choose to share with the group). Use this to record your own observations, questions, and evolving view as DD progresses.

**AngelAI**
Your AI due diligence assistant. Ask it questions about the deal, the sector, the financials, or anything else relevant to your assessment. See [Using AngelAI](/angels/using-angelai) for guidance.

**Q&A**
The full record of questions asked to the entrepreneur and their responses. Useful for understanding what has already been explored and what answers the company has given.

## What to focus on

Not every part of a project needs equal attention. Focus on:

1. **The summary first** — understand the business before looking at the DD findings
2. **The coverage map** — get a quick read on DD progress and where the gaps are
3. **The work packages most relevant to your expertise** — if you are a finance professional, start with the financial model section. If you have sector expertise, start with market and competition
4. **The Project Lead's notes** — the Project Lead's synthesis of what has been found is often the most efficient starting point for forming a view
5. **The WNTBB summary** — when the Project Lead shares their recommendation, this is the structured argument for or against investing. Read it carefully before the group decision meeting

## Asking good questions

If something in the deal summary or the DD findings raises a question, use AngelAI or the project Q&A to explore it. Don't sit on a concern — raise it with the Project Lead or ask AngelAI to help you think it through.

The best angel investors are not the ones who know everything — they are the ones who ask the right questions.
