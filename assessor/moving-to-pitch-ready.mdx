---
title: Moving a company to pitch-ready
description: How to move an application from Screening to Pitching, and what happens next.
---

## When to move to Pitching

Move an application to Pitching when you are satisfied that:

- The application is sufficiently complete and coherent
- The team is credible enough to present to the group
- The business opportunity is relevant to your group's focus and stage
- There are no obvious disqualifiers (e.g. clear EIS/SEIS ineligibility, a business model that is fundamentally unworkable)

You do not need the company to be perfect. The pitch stage exists precisely to allow the group to ask questions and form a view. You are making a judgment about pitch-readiness, not investment-readiness.

## How to move to Pitching

1. Open the project
2. Complete your screening assessment in the **Screening** tab
3. Click **Move to Pitching** in the project header
5. Confirm the move

The entrepreneur is not automatically notified when a project moves to Pitching. Notification is handled separately — see below.

## Notifying the entrepreneur

Once a project is in the Pitching stage, the admin or Project Lead should contact the entrepreneur to:

- Confirm they have been selected to pitch
- Provide the date, time, and format of the pitch event
- Set out any requirements (slide deck format, time limit, Q&A format)

Use  AngelHub's built in email to notify the entrepreneur. A pitch event management feature is available on the Scale plan — see your admin for details.

## Declining instead

If the application is not suitable, decline it from the project menu. Record a brief reason: this is stored internally for the group's reference.

Common decline reasons:
- Too early stage for the group's focus
- Outside the group's sector or geography
- Application too thin to assess — invited to reapply when further developed
- Business model concerns that make the opportunity unsuitable
- EIS/SEIS eligibility concerns

A declined application moves to the **Declined** archive. The entrepreneur can reapply in the future; declining does not permanently exclude them.

## Handing over to the Project Lead

Once a project has attracted interest at a pitch, the Project Lead takes primary responsibility. As the Assessor, you may be asked to brief the Project Lead on:

- What you found during screening
- Any specific concerns or questions that came up in the Q&A
- Your overall impression of the team and the opportunity

Your screening notes are visible to the Project Lead in the project record, so a formal handover meeting is often not necessary, but a quick conversation can help, particularly if there are nuanced concerns that are hard to capture in notes.
